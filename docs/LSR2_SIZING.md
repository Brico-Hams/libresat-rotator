| Fatigue SF at the operating load (Kf keyway 1.8/1.6; thread 2.8) | 4.1 | 1.71 |
| Fit and play | H7/g6 seat, P9 key: slack ~0.16 deg at the output | thread and pin hole: larger slack, pin shear 141 MPa at peak (MEASURE the pin steel) |

**Decision: turned keyed shaft** (higher margin at the gearbox peak and a defined fit). The M14 rod is acceptable only with a torque limit <= 16.7 N·m (rated, not peak) and an inspection of the roll-pin holes. Keep the M14 rod as the low-cost fallback.

## 14. LSR-WG30: our own worm gearbox (phase 7)

Drop-in for the NMRV030 (same faces, Ø14 keyed hollow output, Ø55 boss, NEMA23 flange, 80:1, same envelope); source in the head: **nmrv030**. Rows of this section gate `--check` only when `GEARBOX_SOURCE = lsr_wg30`; the self-locking row always gates. All allowables are VERIFY (gear maker, catalogue).

| Heat at the worst continuous operating point | 4.7 W loss; 12 K rise continuous, 1 K at the satellite duty (10%) | PARAMETRIC duty |

**Verdict.** A single-stage 80:1 worm at a = 30 mm with the only standard module that fits (m = 0.5) carries about 3.1 N·m (bending, fatigue) and 7.8 N·m (wear), against the 16.7 N·m rating of the bought NMRV030: roughly 5 times short. Capacity grows with the cube of the centre distance; parity needs a ≈ 53 mm (an NMRV050-class envelope, heavier and dearer). A two-stage or coaxial pre-stage cannot fit inside the NMRV030 envelope because the NEMA23 flange axis is the worm axis. The geometry, patterns, bearings and interfaces are complete and drop-in, but with these gears the **LSR-WG30 is an option for light duty (holding at stand-still with a lower wind survival, or a different gear set), not a replacement for the NMRV030 at the loads of this head: keep the bought NMRV030.** The cost comparison is in [LSR2_MATERIALS](LSR2_MATERIALS.md).
